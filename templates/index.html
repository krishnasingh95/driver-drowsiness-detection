Top 10 Viva Questions & Model Answers

Q1. What is Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) and how is it calculated?
Answer: EAR measures eye openness using 6 landmark coordinates per eye. EAR = (||p2 - p6|| + ||p3 - p5||) / (2 * ||p1 - p4||) where p1-p4 is horizontal eye width and p2,p6,p3,p5 are vertical distances. When eyes close, EAR approaches 0.
Q2. Why did you use a 3-Level Risk classification rather than a simple Alert/Drowsy output?
Answer: Human drowsiness is progressive, not binary. Normal blinks last ~0.1 to 0.4s. A binary system would trigger alarms for every natural blink! The 3-level system introduces NORMAL (no action), CAUTION (yawning or partial closure), and HIGH RISK (prolonged closure ≥ 2.0s triggers audio song).
Q3. Why was Personalized Eye Calibration implemented?
Answer: Universal thresholds fail for people with naturally smaller, almond-shaped, or hooded eyes. Calibration records the user's natural baseline EAR for 5 seconds and calculates threshold = baseline × 0.70.
Q4. Why Random Forest instead of a Deep Learning CNN?
Answer: Feature engineering with MediaPipe landmarks reduces video to 5 numerical features. Random Forest runs at >60 FPS on basic CPU without requiring a GPU, prevents overfitting with 200 balanced decision trees, and offers clear feature importance interpretability.
Q5. How does the system play the audio song reliably?
Answer: We implemented a Dual-Audio Engine: the backend uses Python's pygame.mixer to output directly to the laptop's hardware speakers, while the frontend uses HTML5 Web Audio API with automatic interaction unlocking.
Q6. How do you prevent false alarms from camera angle changes?
Answer: Both left and right eyes are computed separately and averaged. MediaPipe 468-point mesh normalizes landmark depth, and the time-closure counter requires continuous frames below threshold rather than a single noisy frame.
